Hardware¶

What to connect between your MCU and a PCA9685, with which bus settings, and the board-level traps to avoid.

All values here come from the PCA9685 datasheet (Rev. 4, 16 April 2015); page numbers refer to it. Pin numbers are for the TSSOP28 package. When they disagree with a breakout board's schematic, the schematic wins.

Pins¶

Pin Signal Function
26 SCL In: I2C clock; pull-up to VDD
27 SDA In/out: I2C data; pull-up to VDD
1-5, 24 A0-A5 In: address bits; no pull-ups, tie each high or low
23 OE In: output enable, active low; no pull-up
25 EXTCLK In: external clock up to 50 MHz; ground it when unused
6-13 LED0-LED7 Out: PWM outputs
15-22 LED8-LED15 Out: PWM outputs
28 VDD Supply, 2.3 V to 5.5 V
14 VSS Ground
HVQFN28 pin numbers

SCL 23, SDA 24, A0 to A4 26, 27, 28, 1, 2, A5 21, OE 20, EXTCLK 22, LED0 to LED7 3 to 10, LED8 to LED15 12 to 19, VDD 25, VSS 11. The exposed pad is also ground and must be soldered.

Source: Table 3, p. 6.

Address¶

The 7-bit address is 1 A5 A4 A3 A2 A1 A0 (Figure 4, p. 8): 0x40 with every address pin low, up to 0x7F with every pin high. Pass it to the constructor: PCA9685<MyI2c> pwm(&bus, 0x41).

Address Use it? Why
0x40 to 0x6F Yes Regular addresses
0x70 No LED All Call address, enabled at power-up: every PCA9685 on the bus answers it
0x71 to 0x77 With care 0x71, 0x72 and 0x74 are the Sub Call defaults (disabled at power-up)
0x78 to 0x7F No, on a shared bus Reserved by the I2C specification (10-bit addressing, future use)

The datasheet counts 62 usable addresses (section 7.1, p. 7). The general-call Software Reset (0x00, data byte 0x06) resets every PCA9685 on the bus; the driver does not send it.

The driver writes a channel as one four-byte transaction starting at LEDn_ON_L and reads single registers. Your port turns each call into exactly one transaction (Porting).

Output stage¶

The 16 outputs share one configuration in MODE2 (Table 6, p. 16).

MODE2 bit Default Effect Driver call
OUTDRV (bit 2) 1: totem pole 0: open drain SetOutputDriverMode(bool totem_pole)
INVRT (bit 4) 0: not inverted 1: outputs inverted while OE is low SetOutputInvert(bool invert)
OCH (bit 3) 0: change on STOP 1: change on ACK none
OUTNE[1:0] (bits 1:0) 00: LEDn = 0 while OE is high 01: 1 (totem pole) or high-Z; 1x: high-Z none

Totem pole sinks 25 mA and sources 10 mA at 5 V; open drain sinks 25 mA. LEDs with built-in Zener diodes must be driven in open-drain mode (Table 6, note 1). For a servo signal or a logic-level gate driver input use totem pole, INVRT = 0.

OE is active low. Low: outputs follow their registers. High: outputs take the OUTNE state, by default low. The driver does not drive OE; tie it low or control it from a GPIO yourself.

To repeat a power-on reset, VDD must fall below 0.2 V (section 7.5, p. 27). Place a 100 nF capacitor between VDD and VSS close to the chip. Loads (servos, LED strings, motors) take their own supply; only the signal and the ground are shared with the PCA9685.

Breakout boards¶

The ESP32 examples run on an ESP32-S3 with a PCA9685 at 0x40: SDA on GPIO4, SCL on GPIO5, 100 kHz, OE not driven. The pins are in esp32_pca9685_test_config.hpp. Common 16-channel servo breakouts fit the I2C pull-ups, a pull-down on OE and solder jumpers for A0 to A5; check the board's schematic.
